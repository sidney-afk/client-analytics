# Speed map — every tab, first load and switching

**Date:** 2026-09-23 · **Session:** Gauge (supervisor: Lighthouse)
**Method:** `docs/audits/2026-09-23-boot-baseline.md` §4, extended. **Measurement only:
no app code changed, no writes to any backend, test client only.**

Companion to the boot baseline, which measured two views. This measures all fifteen
reachable tabs, how long each takes when it is the first page loaded, how long it takes
to *switch* to it from the calendar, what it waits on before showing anything, and how
three common interactions feel once it is open.

---

## 1. Headline

- **Tab speed in SyncView is set by what a tab fetches on entry, not by how much
  JavaScript the page carries.** TikTok pilot and TikTok upload ship in the *same two
  fragments*. Pilot is usable in 590 ms and switches in 25 ms; upload takes 2,728 ms and
  switches in 3,527 ms. Same code, same bytes, 140× difference in switching.
- **Switching — what staff do all day — costs seconds on the three busiest tabs**, on an
  already-loaded page where no document is downloaded at all: **Workload 4,284 ms,
  SyncLinear 3,996 ms, TikTok upload 3,527 ms.** Templates, Submit, TikTok pilot and
  Samples all switch in under 30 ms.
- **Two tabs are broken, not slow.** Reloading Kasper **hiring** never brings it back
  (6 of 6 loads across two independent passes). Kasper **onboarding** comes back 2 times
  in 6, and takes ~5.5 s when it does.
- **Analytics is the 4th slowest tab, not the fastest.** An early reading of 130 ms was
  measuring boot-skeleton markup. Measured against real content it is **5,116 ms warm**.
- **Several tabs are held open by data they never display.** Kasper review fires 51
  requests before first content and the last four to finish — at 10.8 s — are Google
  Sheets tabs and an n8n webhook that have nothing to do with the review queue.
- **Every tab pays a fixed entry tax** of roughly **172 KB across ~20 requests**, gated
  behind `key-verify` and two separate `syncview_runtime_flags` reads, before any tab's
  own data is even requested.

**Consequence for the roadmap.** `docs/plans/2026-09-21-post-modularization-roadmap.md`
phase C splits rarely used code out of the 1.4 MB document, projected at 150–350 ms off a
*first* visit. Nothing in this map is limited by document bytes. **Code splitting cannot
improve any of the top five tabs**, because every one of them is bound by per-entry data
fetching that a warm cache already eliminates the download for.

## 2. What was measured, and what is trustworthy

Same driver shape as the boot baseline §4: Playwright, Chromium, 1440×950, the live site,
a persistent staff profile the owner signed into by hand, readiness probes installed via
`addInitScript`, cold via CDP `Network.clearBrowserCache` + `setCacheDisabled`, warm via a
full reload, an `about:blank` hop before every cold load so a `#fragment`-only `goto()`
can never be a same-document no-op, and the `index.html` transfer size cross-checked
against the cold/warm label on every single load.

**Two full passes were run.** They disagreed, and the disagreement is a finding:

- **Warm loads reproduce.** Warm medians agree within ~10–25% across independent passes
  (Workload 6,219 / 6,008 / 6,135 ms; calendar 787 / 815 ms; templates 606 / 585 ms;
  submit 576 / 622 ms).
- **Cold loads do not.** Cold medians differ by 2–4× between passes on the same tabs
  (Workload 13,471 vs 8,358 vs 5,994 ms; Kasper review 3,749 vs 8,327 ms; Kasper hiring
  2,280 vs 10,048 ms).

**So the ranking below is by warm median.** Cold is reported only as an observed range,
explicitly not as a reproducible figure. Warm is also the state staff are in all day,
which is what this task asked about. Every figure pools only loads that passed the byte
cross-check — `docTx === 1,404,287` to count as cold, `=== 300` to count as warm — which
silently discarded a set of cache-contaminated Kasper-onboarding cold loads rather than
letting them skew a median.

Readiness selectors were derived live per tab and are recorded in §3. They are **not**
interchangeable: Samples reuses the calendar's `.cal-card`, so both are scoped to their
own container.

## 4. Switching from the calendar — the number that matters most

Land on the calendar, wait until it is usable, then click the tab's real nav element and
time until that tab's content is visible. No document is downloaded; the app is already
running. 3 reps each, median reported.

| tab | reps (ms) | **median** |
|---|---|---|
| **Workload** | 4365, 4284, 4182 | **4,284** |
| **SyncLinear** | 2086, 3996, 4333 | **3,996** |
| **TikTok upload** | 3926, 3382, 3527 | **3,527** |
| Kasper review | 784, 609, 778 | **778** |
| Filming Plans | 574, 650, 615 | **615** |
| TikTok pilot | 25, 35, 24 | **25** |
| Submit | 23, 18, 21 | **21** |
| Samples | 19, 146, 15 | **19** |
| Templates | 8, 11, 9 | **9** |

Workload's switch times are strikingly *tight* (4,182–4,365 ms) while its cold loads
sprawl from 5.3 s to 30.6 s. On an already-booted page its entry cost is a steady ~4.3 s.

**Samples' 19 ms was verified rather than assumed.** A dedicated probe confirmed
`#sxrView` did not exist before the click, 0 cards before, 3 cards after, switch 12 ms.
It genuinely renders from data already in memory.

Time off, SMM reports and the two Kasper subtabs have no top-level nav button and were not
switch-tested.

Three distinct problems, and they need different fixes:

1. **Data a tab does not display, on its critical path.** Kasper review waits on
   `Competitor Briefs`, `Social Media Managers`, `TopVideos` and `filming-plan-tabs` —
   none of which are the review queue. Workload waits on `TopVideos`, a video analytics
   spreadsheet. TikTok upload waits on `Metrics`.
2. **The same query issued repeatedly in one boot.** SyncLinear fetches
   `production_deliverables_browser_v` **three times** (4,136 / 3,737 / 2,841 ms). That is
   a duplicate-request problem, not a payload problem.
3. **A fixed entry tax on every tab.** The three fastest tabs show an identical floor —
   20 requests, 172,241 bytes — gated behind `key-verify` plus two separate
   `syncview_runtime_flags` reads. No tab can start before that clears.

## 6. How the top tabs feel once open

Three interactions per priority tab, 3 reps each. Completion is measured as **the visible
UI changed, then stopped changing** — a signature of every visible element plus rendered
text length, polled on `requestAnimationFrame` — rather than a guessed selector appearing.
Two numbers: time to first visible change, and time to settle.

| tab | action | first change | settled |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workload | open a plan item | 17–20 ms | 89–92 ms |
| Workload | open a day chip | 6–8 ms | 72 ms |
| Workload | open a loose item | — | **no visible change** |
| SyncLinear | open an issue row | 47–48 ms | 104–115 ms |
| SyncLinear | open row context menu | 71–116 ms | 360–408 ms |
| SyncLinear | click second row | 46–48 ms | 99–114 ms |
| Calendar | open a card | 11–18 ms | **never settles** |
| Calendar | open Organize menu | 4–12 ms | **never settles** |
| Calendar | switch calendar tab | 10–18 ms | **never settles** |

**Interactions are not the problem.** Once a tab is open, everything responds in tens of
milliseconds and settles well under half a second. The cost in this app is entry, not use.

Two observations that are leads, not conclusions:

- **The calendar never settles**, on all 9 interaction reps: something in its visible DOM
  repaints continuously for the full 10 s measurement window. The first visible response
  is fast (4–18 ms), so this is not a slow interaction — it is continuous repainting.
  The likeliest candidate is the persistent "Refreshing…" indicator visible in the
  calendar's rendered text. **Not diagnosed here**; worth its own look, since perpetual
  repaint costs battery and can make an otherwise fast screen feel unsettled.
- **Workload's "loose item" produced no visible change at all** on 3 of 3 reps. It may
  simply not be interactive; not investigated.

## 7. Correctness finding — Kasper subtabs do not survive a reload

This is a bug, not a speed result. It surfaced here only because a speed map reloads
things.

| entry | cold | warm (reload of the same URL) |
|---|---|---|
| `?Kasper=1#kasper` (base tab) | works, 6/6 | **works**, 2,401 ms median |
| `?Kasper=1#kasper/hiring-process` | works, 6/6 | **never renders, 6/6** |
| `?Kasper=1#kasper/onboarding` | works, 6/6 | **null 4/6**; ~5.2–5.7 s when it recovers |

The base Kasper tab reloads fine; both *subtab* deep links do not. The boot router in
`005-head-boot.html.part` does read a subtab out of the hash and set `data-boot-subtab`,
but `_kasperFallbackToReview()` (`320-kasper-dashboard-replies.js.part`) can override the
subtab once `_syncviewStaffCan()` resolves — which is consistent with hiring (admin-only,
deterministic failure) failing every time and onboarding (admin/smm/creative, a wider
gate) failing intermittently.

**Not root-caused here** — that needs a code reading, not a stopwatch. What is
established is the behaviour and its consistency: **staff who refresh or bookmark the
hiring tab do not get the hiring tab back.**

## 8. Top five — what would fix each

> **Proposals, not measurements.** Nothing in this section has been implemented or
> verified. The measured material is §3 to §7.

### 1. Kasper hiring never returns on reload *(correctness, do first — it is cheapest and it is broken)*
Make the subtab restore survive the capability gate resolving: re-apply the hash's
subtab after `_syncviewStaffCan()` settles, instead of letting `_kasperFallbackToReview()`
win unconditionally. Projected: turns a never into a normal ~2.4 s Kasper load. Smallest
change on this list and the only one that fixes a broken behaviour rather than a slow one.

### 2. Workload — 6,008 ms warm, 4,284 ms to switch
Two independent changes:
- **Take `sheets:TopVideos` off Workload's critical path.** A video analytics spreadsheet
  finishing at 6,955 ms, immediately before the workload snapshot at 6,939 ms, is not
  something the workload board needs in order to draw.
- **Render the board from the snapshot as it arrives** rather than awaiting the whole
  2 MB answer. OPEN_REPAIRS 230 documents the 8 s `AbortController` around
  `wlFetchNativeSnapshot`; the fix there raised the budget, which makes the failure rarer
  without making the wait shorter.
Projected: most of the ~4.3 s switch cost, since the switch case has no download at all.

### 3. Kasper onboarding — intermittent, 5,454 ms when it works
Same root as #1 (a gate race), plus its own entry cost. Fixing #1 likely fixes the
intermittency; the residual ~5.5 s is the Kasper entry payload, which #5 addresses.

### 4. Analytics — 5,116 ms warm
Unmeasured in composition: the remediation driver captured its timing but not its
blocking requests, so the specific blockers are **not known**. Given the tab is the client
analytics overview and the Sheets pulls (`Metrics`, `TopVideos`, `Clients Info`) dominate
elsewhere, the first step is to measure its blockers, then apply the same treatment as
§5.1 — render the grid from whatever has arrived and let the rest fill in. **Do not act on
this one before measuring it.**

### 5. SyncLinear — 2,822 ms warm, 3,996 ms to switch, 1,573 rows
- **Stop fetching `production_deliverables_browser_v` three times per boot.** One request
  at 4,136 ms, a second at 3,737 ms, a third at 2,841 ms, in a single load.
- **Do not build 1,573 `.prod-row` elements before showing anything.** Render the first
  screenful and continue. This is a render cost, so unlike the others it would also be
  helped by doing less work up front rather than by fetching less.

### Cross-cutting, worth more than any single tab
**The ~172 KB / 20-request entry tax gates every tab**, including the three that are
otherwise near-instant, behind `key-verify` and two separate `syncview_runtime_flags`
reads. Collapsing the two flag reads into one and letting tabs render before `key-verify`
resolves (it is a paint hint, not the authority — the real gate is server-side, per the
comment in `005-head-boot.html.part`) would lift the floor under all fifteen tabs at once.

## 9. Limits of this measurement

- **Cold-load times are not reproducible on this rig** (2–4× between passes). Ranking is
  by warm median. Re-measure warm on the same rig after any change; do not compare cold
  numbers across sessions or machines.
- **Analytics' blocking requests were not captured**, only its timing. Kasper
  onboarding's blocking data was lost to the cache-contamination filter.
- **Three reps per tab** for switching and actions; 3–11 qualifying loads per tab for
  first load. Enough to separate 4,284 ms from 25 ms; not enough for ±10% claims.
- **One machine, one connection, one evening, one staff account, one test client.** The
  test client's calendar holds 17–18 posts and 3 sample cards; a client with ten times
  that may behave differently, and SyncLinear's 1,573 rows are workspace-wide rather than
  client-scoped.
- **The calendar's "never settles" result is a lead, not a diagnosis.**
- The client link's Google Sheets wait is being fixed on another branch and was
  deliberately not touched here.

## 10. Reproduction

Drivers ran from a scratchpad and are not committed: they take URLs from the environment,
and the client-facing URL carries a share token and a client display name that do not
belong in a public repo. The method in §2, plus the selectors in §3, is enough to rebuild
them. The staff profile used for these runs was deleted at the end of the session.
